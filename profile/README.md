## Overview
Oracle Micros Simphony — often just called Micros Simphony — is a point-of-sale platform for restaurants and hospitality venues, handling everything from taking orders and processing payments to kitchen display coordination and enterprise-wide reporting. It runs on Windows-based POS terminals at the point of sale, while back-office management and reporting are accessible through a web browser, letting a single restaurant or a large multi-location group manage operations from one platform.

## Features
- Order entry and payment processing at the point of sale
- Kitchen display system coordination for order routing
- Enterprise reporting across single or multiple locations
- Menu, pricing, and promotion management from a central back office
- Micros Simphony integration with property management, loyalty, and payment systems

## Installation
1. Micros Simphony isn't a self-service download — Oracle Hospitality or an authorized partner sets up and configures the system for your location.
2. POS terminals are installed and configured on-site as part of that setup.
3. Staff and managers then sign in — at the terminal for POS work, and through a browser for back-office and reporting access.

## Getting Started
Once Micros Simphony is live, staff typically complete short training on order entry and payment workflows at the terminal, while managers get access to the back-office tools for menus, pricing, and reporting. Ongoing use centers on taking orders, monitoring the kitchen display, and reviewing sales and performance reports as the business runs.

## FAQ
**What is Oracle Micros Simphony?**
It's Oracle's restaurant point-of-sale and management platform, covering order taking, payments, kitchen coordination, and reporting.

**Can Micros Simphony integrate with other systems?**
Yes — Micros Simphony integration is a core part of the platform, connecting with property management systems, payment processors, loyalty programs, and other hospitality technology.

**Is Micros Simphony only for large restaurant chains?**
No — it's used by single-location restaurants as well as large multi-site hospitality groups.
